Overview

One system for every car

CNR Car Zone (formerly RKS Motors, rebranded in 2026) runs the day-to-day operations of a pre-owned vehicle business. It follows each car through sourcing, mechanic inspection, negotiation, purchase, payments, photos and documents, through to ready-for-sale.

It is built for one owner, used on a phone, and in production today.

Responsibilities

What I owned

All of it: the product decisions, the interface, the data model, the build and the release process.

  • Product owner: what each phase ships, and what waits for the next one.
  • Designer: a mobile-first interface built around the jobs waiting to be done.
  • Engineer: Next.js 15 on Supabase and Postgres, 13 tables protected by row-level security.
  • Release manager: separate staging and production environments with a branch-based release.

Challenge

A long process, real money, private data

  • A multi-step process with inspections, offers, payments, photos and paperwork for every vehicle, which all has to be visible at a glance.
  • Sensitive records. Seller IDs and bank details live in the system and must never leak through an export.
  • A live business. Changes cannot risk the owner's real data, and the schema has to evolve without breaking records that already exist.

Process

Shipped in phases

Phase 1A · Shipped

The core operations loop

Opportunity intake with auto-numbering, mechanic inspection, dated negotiation offers, one-way conversion into inventory with a unique stock number, purchase and payment tracking with receipts, a 22-category photo pipeline, an 18-item document checklist, dashboard, global search, a pending-work queue and CSV exports.

Phase 2 · Shipped

Multi-image documents, staging split and rebrand

Documents hold multiple captioned images, backwards-compatible with every existing record. Live and staging environments separated with a branch-based release. A full rebrand from RKS Motors to CNR Car Zone, wired to CSS variables.

Phase 1B · Next

Sales flow and profit reporting

A sold status and sale record (buyer, date, price, payments received, delivery), with profit calculated against purchase, payments and repair costs, on the dashboard and as an inventory filter.

Decisions I'd make again

  • Row-level security in Postgres. Access rules live in the database, across all 13 tables, rather than only in the interface.
  • A one-way conversion. An opportunity becomes inventory exactly once, with its own stock number, so the record of a purchase cannot be accidentally reopened as a lead.
  • Backwards-compatible schema changes. Multi-image documents shipped without breaking a single existing record.
  • Staging before production. A branch-based release, so nothing untested reaches the owner's real data.
  • Brand in CSS variables. The RKS Motors to CNR Car Zone rebrand made future colour changes a one-file edit.
  • Exports that leave things out. CSV downloads for opportunities, inventory, sellers, payments and documents deliberately exclude seller ID and bank details.

Outcome

Running a real business

CNR Car Zone is in production for a single-owner business. Phases 1A and 2 have shipped; sales and profit reporting is next.

There are no traffic or revenue numbers here, because this is an internal tool for one owner, and I would rather show what it does than invent a metric for it.

Lessons

What building it solo taught me

  • Phase the work by what the business needs next. The core loop came first; reporting waits until there is data worth reporting on.
  • Plan for the schema you will have later. Keeping old records valid is what let multi-image documents ship without a migration headache.
  • Treat privacy as a feature. Deciding what an export leaves out is as much a design decision as what it includes.
